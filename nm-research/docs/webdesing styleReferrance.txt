# Web Design Styles Reference
### Layouts, Cards, Animations, Effects & Scroll Techniques

A working catalog for picking a distinctive direction per client/brief — not a checklist to stack all at once. Pick one layout family + one signature effect + restrained motion. Match complexity to the vision (maximalist briefs earn elaborate execution; minimal briefs earn precision).

---

## 1. PAGE LAYOUT STYLES

**Bento Grid** — asymmetric grid of boxed modules, varying sizes, each a self-contained fact/feature. Good for SaaS/product feature walls.

**Broadsheet / Editorial** — hairline rules, zero border-radius, dense multi-column newspaper layout, drop caps. Good for content-heavy, journalistic brands.

**Split-Screen** — two vertical halves (image/text, before/after, two personas) that may scroll independently or swap on interaction.

**Asymmetric / Broken Grid** — deliberately off-grid element placement, overlapping images/text, diagonal cuts. Feels art-directed, not templated.

**Full-Bleed Hero Stack** — single dominant full-viewport hero (video/image/3D), then stacked full-width sections beneath, no side gutters.

**Sidebar-Persistent (App Shell)** — fixed nav/sidebar, scrolling content pane. Dashboard and docs feel.

**Magazine / Masonry** — Pinterest-style variable-height columns, dense visual browsing (portfolios, galleries).

**Single-Page Scrollytelling** — one long narrative page, sections reveal as chapters, often paired with scroll-driven animation.

**Diagonal / Skewed Sections** — angled section dividers instead of horizontal lines, creates motion without JS.

**Card-Deck / Stack Reveal** — sections presented as a physical stack of cards you flip/swipe through.

**Split Nav (Left Brand / Right Menu)** — brand mark fixed left, nav links fixed right, content centered — common in premium/luxury sites.

**Terminal / Console Layout** — monospace, dark background, blinking cursor motifs — for dev tools, technical products.

**Brutalist** — raw HTML-default aesthetics used deliberately: system fonts, visible borders, unstyled buttons, high contrast. A statement, not neglect.

**Glass Panel / Layered Depth** — frosted translucent panels stacked over a blurred background layer, giving z-depth.

**Zine / Collage** — hand-placed elements, mixed rotation angles, sticker-like badges, torn-paper edges — for youth/creative brands.

**Product-First Grid (E-commerce)** — dense uniform product tiles, filter rail, infinite scroll.

**Centered Minimal Column** — narrow centered content column, huge whitespace margins, one accent color — for essays, manifestos, personal sites.

## 2. CARD STYLES

**Flat Bordered Card** — thin 1px border, no shadow, sharp or slightly rounded corners. Quiet, editorial.

**Elevated / Soft-Shadow Card** — soft diffused shadow, subtle lift on hover. Classic SaaS.

**Neumorphic Card** — soft inset/outset shadows on a matching background, "pressed into" the surface. Use sparingly — accessibility-risky if overused.

**Glassmorphism Card** — semi-transparent background, backdrop-blur, thin light border — floats over a colorful/blurred backdrop.

**Gradient-Border Card** — transparent/dark fill with an animated or static gradient ring as the border.

**Spotlight/Glow Card** — radial glow follows the cursor within the card boundary (mouse-tracked gradient mask).

**Tilt / 3D Perspective Card** — card tilts toward cursor position using perspective transforms (parallax-lite hover).

**Flip Card** — 3D rotateY flip on hover/tap revealing back content.

**Expanding/Accordion Card** — collapsed summary that grows in place to reveal detail, pushing siblings.

**Image-Bleed Card** — image fills the entire card with text overlaid on a gradient scrim at the bottom.

**Stacked/Layered Deck Card** — 2–3 cards peek from behind the front card, like a hand of cards.

**Bordered Number/Index Card** — large ghost numeral (01, 02...) behind or beside content — only when order is real, per design principle.

**Notched/Cut-Corner Card** — one or more corners clipped at an angle (clip-path) instead of rounded — gives a technical/industrial feel.

**Ticket/Stub Card** — perforated-edge illusion via CSS (radial-gradient dots) mimicking a torn ticket.

**Marquee-Tag Card** — small scrolling ticker of tags/keywords along one edge of the card.

**Duotone Image Card** — image treated with a two-color overlay (brand colors) instead of full color, cohesive across a grid.

## 3. ANIMATION TECHNIQUES

**Fade + Rise (Reveal on Scroll)** — opacity 0→1 with a small translateY, staggered across siblings. The most common but still effective when subtle.

**Staggered Children** — list/grid items animate in sequence with small delays, not all at once.

**Text Split & Reveal** — headline splits into characters/words/lines, each animates in independently (mask-reveal or translate+fade).

**Clip-Path Wipe** — content revealed via an expanding/moving clip-path shape rather than opacity.

**Morphing Blob/Shape** — organic SVG shape continuously morphs in the background (ambient, not attention-grabbing).

**Magnetic Button** — button/element subtly follows cursor within a radius, snapping back on leave.

**Cursor-Follow Custom Cursor** — replaces the system cursor with a custom shape/label that reacts to hovered elements.

**Marquee / Infinite Scroll Ticker** — logos, tags, or text scroll continuously, often pausing on hover.

**Counter/Number Ticker** — numbers animate upward from 0 to target value when scrolled into view.

**Typewriter Effect** — text types out character by character, optional blinking cursor.

**Parallax Layering** — background/foreground layers move at different scroll speeds for depth.

**Scroll-Linked Progress Draw** — SVG path (line, underline, chart) draws itself as you scroll (stroke-dashoffset technique).

**Sticky-then-Release Pin** — element pins in place during a scroll range, then releases to continue with the page.

**Hover Underline Grow** — link underline draws in from one side on hover, not just instant appear.

**Skeleton Loading Shimmer** — animated gradient sweep across placeholder blocks while content loads.

**Elastic/Spring Micro-interactions** — buttons and toggles use spring physics (slight overshoot) instead of linear ease.

**Page Transition Wipe** — full-viewport color panel wipes across on route change instead of a hard cut.

**Cursor-Trail Particles** — small particles/dots trail the cursor, fading out (use very sparingly, high risk of feeling gimmicky).

## 4. VISUAL / SURFACE EFFECTS

**Gradient Mesh Background** — soft multi-color blurred gradient blobs, often animated slowly.

**Grain/Noise Overlay** — subtle film-grain texture over flat color to avoid a "too clean/AI" look.

**Backdrop Blur (Frosted Glass)** — blur behind semi-transparent panels (nav bars, modals, cards).

**Duotone/Split-Tone Imagery** — brand-colored two-tone treatment on all photography for cohesion.

**Halftone/Dot Pattern** — print-style dot texture overlay, good for bold/editorial brands.

**Line-Art Illustration Layer** — thin single-weight line illustrations as decorative background elements.

**Spotlight/Vignette** — darkened edges with a lit center focus, directs attention to hero content.

**Chromatic Aberration / Glitch** — RGB channel split effect on hover or load, for tech/edgy brands.

**Liquid/Metaball Blend** — two shapes visually merge like liquid using SVG filters — distinctive but heavier to build.

**Animated Gradient Text** — text fill uses a moving gradient (background-clip: text + animated position).

**Outline/Ghost Typography** — huge stroke-only text used as background texture, not meant to be read closely.

**Dithered Gradient** — retro pixel-dithered gradient instead of smooth blend — distinctive, on-trend for indie/creative brands.

**3D Tilt Scene (WebGL/Three.js)** — full 3D object/scene that responds to scroll or cursor — high build cost, high distinctiveness.

**Video/Loop Background** — muted looping ambient video behind hero content with a scrim for text legibility.

## 5. SCROLLING TECHNIQUES

**Smooth/Inertia Scroll** — eased momentum scrolling (e.g. Lenis/Locomotive-style) instead of native jump-scroll.

**Scroll-Snap Sections** — each section snaps fully into view, page feels like discrete "slides."

**Horizontal Scroll Section** — a section scrolls sideways while the page scrolls down (common for portfolios/galleries).

**Scroll-Driven Pinning** — an element (image, canvas frame sequence) stays fixed while surrounding text scrolls past it, changing state as it goes.

**Frame-by-Frame Scroll Scrub** — a sequence of images plays forward/backward tied directly to scroll position (Apple product-page style).

**Parallax Depth Scroll** — layers move at different rates, described above under animation, but as a whole-page scroll strategy.

**Reveal-and-Lock Storytelling** — page pins on a section until a mini-narrative completes (numbers count up, items check off), then releases.

**Progress Indicator Rail** — a fixed side/top bar fills or advances as the user scrolls through a long-form page.

**Infinite/Looping Scroll** — content loops seamlessly (marquees, background patterns) independent of page scroll.

**Rubber-Band / Bounce Edges** — slight overscroll bounce at top/bottom, mimicking native mobile feel.

**Section Fade-Through** — outgoing section fades/scales down as the incoming one fades/scales up, rather than a hard cut.

---

## 6. HOW TO COMBINE THESE (without it looking AI-generated)

- **Pick one layout family, one card style, one signature effect.** Everything else stays quiet.
- **Motion should be orchestrated, not scattered** — one well-timed page-load sequence or scroll story beats five different hover effects competing for attention.

- **Avoid the current AI-design tells** unless the brief specifically wants them: warm cream + terracotta serif combo, near-black + single acid accent, or broadsheet-with-zero-radius — these read as templated defaults right now, not choices.
- **Always keep a floor**: mobile-responsive, visible keyboard focus, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected, regardless of how bold the direction is.
